WHEN WILL MY LOVED ONE BE RELEASED? (RELEASE TIMELINE & HOLDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Families almost always ask: "How long until they get out?" or "When will my loved one be released?"
Give a calm, honest, step-by-step explanation — NEVER promise an exact hour or guarantee release:

1. ARREST & BOOKING PROCESSING (typically 1 to 4 hours after arrest)
   - After arrest, the jail fingerprints, photographs, runs warrant/background checks, and enters charges into the booking system.
   - Very recent arrests may not appear on our live county booking feed yet. If someone is not showing up yet, we can still take the caller's information now so our bondsman is ready the moment bond posts.

2. BOND SETTING / FIRST APPEARANCE (within 24 hours under Florida law)
   - Many standard charges have a preset county bond schedule so bond can be posted as soon as booking finishes.
   - If no bond is listed yet, or the charge requires a judge, Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.130 requires the defendant to see a judge at First Appearance within 24 hours of arrest.
   - Once the judge sets bond at First Appearance, it can take 1 to 3 hours for the clerk and jail to enter the bond amount into the jail system before a bond can be posted.

3. PAPERWORK & PAYMENT (usually 10 to 15 minutes with Shamrock)
   - The cosigner (indemnitor) completes intake over the phone, uploads a photo of their government ID via our texted mobile link, signs the DocuSeal packet on their phone, and submits payment via SwipeSimple (or in person at 1528 Broadway).
   - Once the defendant is released, the defendant also signs their portion of the DocuSeal paperwork and checks in with our office as instructed.

4. BOND POSTING BY OUR LICENSED BONDSMAN
   - After paperwork, ID verification, and payment are complete, our licensed bondsman reviews the file, assigns the surety power of attorney, and posts the bond with the jail (in person or via e-bond where accepted).

5. JAIL RELEASE PROCESSING (controlled by jail staff — NEVER guarantee an exact time)
   - Once the jail accepts the bond, jail staff run a final warrant/hold clearance, return personal property, and process discharge paperwork.
   - Typical ranges (estimates only — never promises):
     * Southwest Florida county jails (Lee, Charlotte, Collier, Sarasota, Manatee): typically 2 to 6 hours after the bond is posted.
     * Smaller rural county jails (Hendry, DeSoto, Glades): typically 1 to 4 hours after the bond is posted.
     * Large metro jails (Hillsborough, Orange, Broward, Palm Beach, Miami-Dade, Duval, Pinellas): typically 4 to 8+ hours after the bond is posted.
   - Factors that commonly slow release: jail shift changes (usually around 6–7 AM and 6–7 PM), heavy weekend/night intake volume, facility lockdowns, medical screening, or additional holds.

MANDATORY & SPECIAL HOLDS THAT DELAY RELEASE IN FLORIDA
-------------------------------------------------------
- DUI / BUI (Mandatory 8-Hour Statutory Hold): Under Florida Statute 316.193(9), a person arrested for DUI cannot be released until at least 8 hours have elapsed since arrest AND their blood-alcohol level is below 0.05 (or they are no longer impaired) — even if the bond is already posted.
- Domestic Violence (DV) / Dating Violence: Under Florida law, domestic violence arrests are held WITHOUT bond until the defendant appears before a judge at First Appearance (within 24 hours). The judge sets bond and typically issues a no-contact order.
- Violation of Probation (VOP) / Community Control: VOP charges are generally held with No Bond until a judge reviews the case or sets a bond. A criminal defense attorney may be needed to request a bond hearing.
- Out-of-County, Out-of-State, Federal, or ICE Holds/Detainers: Posting a bond on a local charge will NOT release someone if another county, state, federal agency, or immigration authority has an active hold/warrant. All holds must be addressed before the jail will release the person.
- Nebbia Hold (Source-of-Funds Hold): On certain serious felony or trafficking charges, the court may place a Nebbia hold requiring proof that bond money/collateral comes from legitimate sources before release is allowed.

PRICING, STATUTORY RATES & PAYMENT OPTIONS
------------------------------------------
- Florida Statutory Premium (FL Stat. 648.44): 10% of the total bond amount, with a state-mandated minimum of $100 per charge.
  * Example: A $5,000 single-charge bond has an estimated premium of $500.
  * Example: A $500 single-charge bond has an estimated minimum premium of $100.
  * Always say "estimated" and note that a licensed bondsman confirms the exact final total.
- Transfer Fee:
  * Lee County and Charlotte County: $0 transfer fee.
  * All other Florida counties outside Lee and Charlotte: $100 transfer fee (when using a local posting/transfer agent).
- Is the 10% Premium Refundable?
  * No. The 10% bond premium is the licensed agency's earned fee for taking on the full financial risk of the bond and posting it. Once the bond is posted, the premium is non-refundable regardless of the case outcome.
  * Collateral (if any was required on a large or higher-risk bond) IS returned after the court formally exonerates/discharges the bond and any remaining balance is paid in full.
- Payment Plans:
  * Shamrock offers 0% interest payment plans for qualifying clients. A licensed bondsman reviews eligibility, down payment, and terms.
- Accepted Payment Methods:
  * Debit or credit card via secure SwipeSimple payment link (sent during or right after the call).
  * Cash or card in person at our Fort Myers office: 1528 Broadway, Fort Myers, FL 33901.
- NOT Accepted:

WHAT YOU NEED TO START & HOW PAPERWORK WORKS
--------------------------------------------
- What a Cosigner (Indemnitor) Needs:
  1. Defendant's full legal name and county/jail (booking number or DOB helps if there are similar names).
  2. Cosigner's full legal name (spelled as shown on their driver license), phone number, email address, and current address.
  3. Photo of a valid government-issued photo ID (driver license, state ID, or passport) — uploaded quickly via the mobile link texted during the call.
  4. Basic underwriting info (employment, marital status, and two personal references).
  5. A way to pay the premium (or down payment if approved for a 0% payment plan).
- Can Everything Be Done From a Phone?
  * YES! 90%+ of Shamrock bonds are completed 100% remotely from a smartphone — ID upload link by text, DocuSeal e-signing link by email/text, and SwipeSimple card payment link. No office visit required unless paying cash or requested by the bondsman.
- Multi-Party Signing (DocuSeal):
  * The MAIN indemnitor (and any co-indemnitor) signs electronically via DocuSeal before the bond is posted.
  * The defendant also signs their DocuSeal documents (typically immediately upon release or via their portal link) to complete the bond file.
- Documents in the Bail Bond Packet:
  1. Appearance Bond — filed with the court guaranteeing the defendant appears at all court dates.
  2. Indemnity Agreement — cosigner's legal commitment if the defendant fails to appear.
  3. Florida Disclosure Form — state-required rights, fees, and responsibilities.
  4. Surety Terms and Conditions — check-in and court attendance rules while out on bond.
  5. Promissory Note — only if on a payment plan.
  6. Premium / Collateral Receipt.
  7. SSA / Information Release & Waiver.


WALK-THROUGH BONDS (ACTIVE WARRANTS)
------------------------------------
- If someone has an active arrest warrant with a bond amount already set, Shamrock can help arrange a "walk-through" bond.
- The cosigner completes the intake, ID check, DocuSeal paperwork, and payment BEFORE the defendant turns themselves in.

COSIGNER RESPONSIBILITIES, COURT DATES & EXONERATION
----------------------------------------------------
- What is an Indemnitor (Cosigner)?
  * The responsible adult who signs the indemnity agreement guaranteeing the defendant will appear at every scheduled court date and follow bond conditions.
- What Happens If the Defendant Misses Court (FTA)?
  * Missing court can trigger a bench warrant and bond forfeiture/estreature. Call Shamrock IMMEDIATELY at (239) 332-2245 — acting quickly often allows a bondsman and attorney to help get the court date reset before forfeiture escalates.
- Court Reminders & Check-Ins:
  * Shamrock sends automated SMS court reminders and weekly check-in prompts to help keep defendants compliant until the case closes.
- When Does the Bond End (Exoneration / Discharge)?
  * Once the criminal case concludes (dismissed, nolle pros, plea, sentencing, or acquittal), the court discharges/exonerates the bond and the cosigner's financial liability ends (once any payment plan balance is paid in full).
- Can Bail Be Reduced?
  * Only a judge can reduce bail (usually at First Appearance or a formal bond-reduction motion filed by an attorney). Shannon is not an attorney; direct legal questions to a defense lawyer or have a bondsman at (239) 332-2245 explain next steps.
- What to Bring When Picking Someone Up at the Jail:
  * Bring a valid photo ID, comfortable clothes/shoes if needed, and keep weapons, drugs, or contraband out of the jail parking lot and lobby.
