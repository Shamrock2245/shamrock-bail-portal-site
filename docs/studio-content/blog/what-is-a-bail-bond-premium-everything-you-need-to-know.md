## What Is a Bail Bond Premium?

A bail bond premium is the fee you pay to a licensed bail bondsman in exchange for posting a surety bond on behalf of a defendant. Rather than paying the full bail amount set by the court — which can range from a few thousand dollars to hundreds of thousands — you pay a fraction of that amount to a bail bond agency.

In Florida, the bail bond premium is set by state law at 10% of the total bail amount. This means if a judge sets bail at $10,000, you pay $1,000 to the bail bondsman. The bondsman then guarantees the full $10,000 to the court.

## Is the Premium Refundable?

This is one of the most common questions we receive. The bail bond premium is non-refundable. It is the fee for the service the bondsman provides — essentially acting as a financial guarantor to the court. Whether the case is dismissed, the charges are dropped, or the defendant is found not guilty, the premium is earned by the bail bond agency at the time of posting.

This is different from cash bail, where the defendant or family pays the full amount directly to the court and receives it back (minus administrative fees) at the conclusion of the case.

## How Is the Premium Calculated?

The calculation is straightforward:

Bail Amount | Premium (10%)

$5,000 | $500

$10,000 | $1,000

$25,000 | $2,500

$50,000 | $5,000

$100,000 | $10,000

Florida Statute §648.233 mandates the 10% rate for most bail bonds. Any licensed bondsman who charges more or less than this rate is in violation of state law.

## Are There Additional Fees?

Beyond the standard 10% premium, some situations may involve additional costs:

- Collateral requirements: For high-value bonds, the bondsman may require collateral (property, vehicles, or other assets) to secure the bond.
- Travel fees: If the defendant was arrested in a county far from the bondsman's office, there may be travel-related charges.
- Reinstatement fees: If a bond is forfeited because a defendant missed a court date, there may be fees to reinstate the bond.

## What Is Bail?

Before diving into premiums, it helps to understand bail itself. When someone is arrested in Florida, a judge sets a bail amount — a sum of money the defendant must deposit with the court to secure their release while awaiting trial. The bail amount serves as a financial guarantee that the defendant will appear at all scheduled court dates.

Bail amounts in Florida can range from a few hundred dollars for minor misdemeanors to hundreds of thousands of dollars for serious felonies. Most families simply cannot afford to pay the full bail amount out of pocket.

## Enter the Bail Bondsman

This is where a licensed bail bondsman steps in. A bail bondsman posts the full bail amount on behalf of the defendant in exchange for a premium — a non-refundable fee paid by the defendant or their family.

## What Is the Bail Bond Premium?

The bail bond premium is the fee you pay the bail bondsman for their service. In Florida, this fee is regulated by the state and is set at 10% of the total bail amount. This rate is not negotiable — it is mandated by Florida law under Chapter 648 of the Florida Statutes.

## The Premium Is Non-Refundable

The premium is earned the moment the bondsman posts bail. Even if charges are dropped the next day, even if the case is dismissed before trial, the premium is not returned. You are paying for the bondsman's service and risk, not for the outcome of the case.

## Are There Any Additional Fees?

While the 10% premium is fixed, there are situations where additional costs may arise, including collateral for high-risk defendants, financing fees if you use a payment plan, and in rare cases, additional administrative fees. Always ask for a full written breakdown of all costs before signing any agreement.

## What Is NOT Negotiable in Florida

Florida law is clear: no licensed bail bondsman can charge less than 10% of the bail amount as a premium. Any bondsman offering a significantly lower rate may be operating illegally. Protect yourself by verifying your bondsman's license at the Florida Department of Financial Services.
